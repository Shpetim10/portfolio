<Chapter id="problem">

Planning events at a venue like the Pyramid meant juggling emails, phone calls and spreadsheets across organizers, venue staff and suppliers. Requests got lost, bookings clashed, and nobody had a single view of the plan.

</Chapter>

<Chapter id="constraints">

48 hours, a team of five, a new problem domain and a jury expecting a working demo. We also had to visualize the physical venue and handle overlapping events correctly.

</Chapter>

<Chapter id="architecture">

A Next.js web application on a Node.js backend, organized around the event lifecycle: request intake, venue matching, proposal creation, equipment booking and task coordination. A scheduling check catches conflicts between overlapping events, and a 3D mockup of the Pyramid helps organizers picture the space.

<Chapter id="decisions">

Cover the whole event lifecycle rather than one step in depth, so the jury could see the full value in a short demo.

Treat scheduling conflicts as a core rule, checking for overlaps whenever a venue or piece of equipment is booked.

Build a 3D mockup of the venue, so venue matching felt concrete instead of abstract.

</Chapter>
